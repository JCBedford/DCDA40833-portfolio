Semester Reflection

What I learned: Over the course of the semester, I developed practical skills in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Tableau, Python, and Folium. More importantly, I learned how to evaluate the tools I use — whether that is an AI assistant, a visualization platform, or a mapping library — with a critical eye toward both capability and ethics.

Challenges I overcame: Early on, I accidentally unpublished my GitHub Pages site and had to troubleshoot how to get it back online. Working with Tableau's data types required patience and manual adjustment. Learning Mapbox Studio and Folium from scratch for Lab 6 had a steep learning curve, but breaking the assignment into steps (with AI assistance) made it manageable.

How I grew: My understanding of digital culture and data analytics evolved as I learned to navigate complex datasets, design meaningful visualizations, and build a fully functioning website. I moved from being someone who simply consumed data to someone who can present and critique it.

Looking ahead: I will apply these skills in future projects — especially the ability to combine Criminal Justice data with interactive visualization tools. The web development foundation I built here will serve me in any career that requires communicating complex information to a broad audience.

The most important lesson from this course is that tools are only as good as the judgment behind them. AI can scaffold, Tableau can visualize, and code can build — but critical thinking is what turns raw output into meaningful communication.
